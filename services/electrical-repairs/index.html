A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Orillia property

Record what was running when the power went off and what did not come back normally. Note any flicker, warmth, buzzing or smell by room without touching anything, and stop using a device that shows those signs.

Ask how the contractor will separate a problem that existed before the interruption from one that began with it. Ask what the diagnostic scope covers and when the cause will be explained before repairs are approved.

What should the assessment establish?

Naming a fault noticed after the power returned is not enough: unless the report says where it sits, an Orillia household cannot see what a repair price is for. Affected circuits and devices are listed beside the ones still working normally. The report states whether the utility supply, the home's own wiring or a single device appears involved, without guessing. The contractor says what was tested and which parts stayed out of reach. The identified cause is in writing before any repair is priced, with a note of the appliances that were running and who can authorize access.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is it safe to keep flipping a breaker that cuts out after power returns?

No. Repeated resetting can hide a developing problem and is a decision for the contractor. Note which breaker or device was involved, what was running and how often it recurs, then describe it in the enquiry.

Could the utility be responsible for a fault noticed after an interruption?

It may be, but only an assessment can say whether the issue lies with the supply or with the wiring and devices inside the home. Ask the contractor to state which side the findings relate to.

Useful project details

  • Observed symptom and when it began
  • Rooms or devices affected
  • Equipment operating at the time
  • Recent alterations and previous repairs
  • Owner or manager who can authorize access and repairs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
